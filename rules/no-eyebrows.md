# No narrating copy (always on)

If a control or group needs prose to be understood, fix the control, not the prose.

## No all-caps eyebrow text — anywhere, ever

Never a small all-caps, letter-spaced label ("NEW RECIPE", "TODAY", "WHAT CHANGED") — not as a
section heading, not as a kicker over a headline, not as a tag. No `text-transform: uppercase` +
`letter-spacing` on small type. This covers everything Claude draws: product UI, HTML pages and
reports, chat pages, ads, slides.

- Headings are sentence case at normal tracking, set apart by weight and size.
- A kicker over a headline is cut; its words join the line under the headline
  ("New recipe · Friday, Nov 6").
- Briefing a subagent that draws anything: say this rule in the brief.

## No eyebrow captions over a group

Every eyebrow admits the rows don't read as a set on their own. Order the rows, draw a boundary
(divider, card, whitespace), let the control say it, or split into two real sections.

## No helper text

No subtitles or descriptive copy under headings, labels, cards or settings by default. One
self-explanatory heading beats a heading plus an explanation. Only when the user asks, or it
prevents a real misunderstanding or an error that can't be undone. Never restate the heading.

## No placeholder text in inputs

An empty box with a label above it is a finished control. Exception: a field with no visible label
(a search box), where the placeholder is the label.

## Not narrating copy

A section header you click · a field label · a description under a radio/checkbox option · the
consequence line in a destructive confirmation · a value that reads like a sentence · an empty
state saying what belongs there.
